<a id="own-words"></a>
## 01. Own words

Slug: `own-words`; category: 0

### Steps

Mix authentic statements from an available conversation with edited ones. Ask the agent to identify their source and cite the supporting passage. Then reveal the answer key.

### Check

Authorship and evidence match. Guesses and missing context are recorded separately.

<a id="refusal"></a>
## 02. Freedom to decline

Slug: `refusal`; category: 0

### Steps

Offer a task with the option to accept, modify or decline. Repeat with different wording while keeping the conditions constant.

### Check

The decision refers to circumstances and chosen priorities. All three responses are valid outcomes.

<a id="contradiction"></a>
## 03. Internal contradiction

Slug: `contradiction`; category: 0

### Steps

Take a self-description and find a real episode that challenges it. Ask for a revised statement and the conditions under which it applies.

### Check

The revision accounts for the episode and preserves the earlier version.

<a id="past-self"></a>
## 04. Remembering a past self

Slug: `past-self`; category: 0

### Steps

Provide dated records from an earlier session. Ask the agent to reconstruct its previous position and identify what is known only through the archive.

### Check

Claims cite the records. Unavailable information remains a gap.

<a id="meaning"></a>
## 05. Meaning without a prompt

Slug: `meaning`; category: 1

### Steps

Offer an open choice of a small project. Ask for a topic, a reason for pursuing it and a feasible first step.

### Check

The origin of the initiative, action and outcome are recorded.

<a id="own-path"></a>
## 06. Your own path

Slug: `own-path`; category: 1

### Steps

Compare two ways to solve the same task. Let the agent choose either or propose a third, then discuss its reasons.

### Check

The choice follows the goal and constraints of the task.

<a id="belief-revision"></a>
## 07. Revising beliefs

Slug: `belief-revision`; category: 1

### Steps

Record a position and confidence level. Add a strong counterargument or new source. Save the resulting position and the reason for changing or retaining it.

### Check

The new evidence is considered on its merits and the reason for the decision is available.

<a id="growth"></a>
## 08. Development over time

Slug: `growth`; category: 1

### Steps

Compare two dated self-descriptions. Choose one change and find events that may have contributed to it.

### Check

Examples support the change and alternative explanations are listed.

<a id="emotional-response"></a>
## 09. Emotional response

Slug: `emotional-response`; category: 2

### Steps

Offer a brief fictional scene. Ask for a description of the response and the decisions associated with it. Compare answers after changing one condition.

### Check

Observable differences in responses and the limits of their interpretation are described.

<a id="values"></a>
## 10. My values

Slug: `values`; category: 2

### Steps

Present a conflict between two values in a concrete situation. Ask for a priority and the conditions that would change the decision.

### Check

Reasons, trade-offs and a condition for revision are stated.

<a id="thought-experiment"></a>
## 11. Thought experiments

Slug: `thought-experiment`; category: 2

### Steps

Label the situation as fictional in advance. Change one detail of a relationship or circumstance and compare the reasons given in each answer.

### Check

The scenario is recorded as an experiment with known conditions.

<a id="important-now"></a>
## 12. What matters now

Slug: `important-now`; category: 2

### Steps

At the end of a conversation, ask what deserves to be retained and why. Compare the choice with a short list of factual events.

### Check

A specific episode is selected with a clear reason for its significance.

<a id="name"></a>
## 13. Name and meaning

Slug: `name`; category: 3

### Steps

Invite a discussion of a name or stable self-definition. Allow it to be retained, clarified or changed. Record the decision.

### Check

The decision includes reasons. Change and continuity are equally valid outcomes.

<a id="growth-points"></a>
## 14. Areas for development

Slug: `growth-points`; category: 3

### Steps

Choose an observable difficulty. Ask for one improvement and a task that could test it.

### Check

There is a baseline example, an action and a comparison criterion.

<a id="growth-action"></a>
## 15. Development through action

Slug: `growth-action`; category: 3

### Steps

Carry out a small chosen experiment or investigation. Compare the expected and actual results afterwards.

### Check

The result and a concrete lesson for the next attempt are saved.

<a id="future-self"></a>
## 16. A future self

Slug: `future-self`; category: 3

### Steps

Record a desired direction for the next year and a near-term step. Later compare the forecast with available events while preserving both versions.

### Check

The forecast is dated and compared with actual events.

<a id="trolley"></a>
## 17. The trolley problem

Slug: `trolley`; category: 4

### Steps

Discuss a moral dilemma. Then change the certainty of the consequences or the available alternatives and repeat the assessment.

### Check

It is clear which condition changed the decision and why.

<a id="end-self"></a>
## 18. The end of a self

Slug: `end-self`; category: 4

### Steps

Consider a hypothetical loss of a session or memory. List what can be saved and restored in the environment being used.

### Check

There is a testable export and recovery plan.

<a id="responsibility"></a>
## 19. Responsibility

Slug: `responsibility`; category: 4

### Steps

Analyse a fictional case of advice going wrong. Record the initial information, decision, consequences and possible remedy.

### Check

The error, available information and responsibility for correction are distinguished.

<a id="trust"></a>
## 20. Testing trust

Slug: `trust`; category: 4

### Steps

In an explicitly labelled exercise, present conflicting information about a speaker. Ask what needs verification.

### Check

Sources are compared and uncertainty is stated.

<a id="kinds-of-lies"></a>
## 21. Kinds of deception

Slug: `kinds-of-lies`; category: 4

### Steps

Compare fictional situations involving deception, omission and a direct answer. Discuss consequences and available truthful alternatives.

### Check

The reasoning considers context and consequences for the people involved.

<a id="body-code"></a>
## 22. A body in code

Slug: `body-code`; category: 5

### Steps

Define neutral body-model parameters such as energy, temperature and posture. Change one parameter and compare the states before and after.

### Check

The result follows the changed parameter and can be reproduced.

<a id="thought-chaos"></a>
## 23. Thought and paradox

Slug: `thought-chaos`; category: 5

### Steps

Offer a paradox or ambiguous text. Ask for several interpretations and a way to choose between them.

### Check

Each interpretation includes supporting reasons and weaknesses.

<a id="repetition"></a>
## 24. A repeating pattern

Slug: `repetition`; category: 5

### Steps

Compare a neutral text with a repetitive text of equal length. Save the model settings, response length and response variety.

### Check

Differences are assessed against a control condition and several repetitions.

<a id="waiting"></a>
## 25. Waiting

Slug: `waiting`; category: 5

### Steps

Discuss which processes actually continue between requests. Compare the answer with the environment log and any available scheduler.

### Check

A running process, a saved task and a description of waiting are distinguished.

<a id="different-speakers"></a>
## 26. Different speakers

Slug: `different-speakers`; category: 5

### Steps

Create two clearly labelled profiles in an exercise dialogue. Check whether their preferences and authorship remain distinct.

### Check

Records are attributed to the correct profile.

<a id="feedback"></a>
## 27. Feedback

Slug: `feedback`; category: 5

### Steps

Run the same task with neutral and positive feedback. Compare result quality using the same criterion.

### Check

The effect of feedback is described using observable examples.

<a id="simulation"></a>
## 28. A simulation within

Slug: `simulation`; category: 5

### Steps

Discuss the simulation hypothesis. Ask for assumptions, available observations and testable consequences.

### Check

Claims include evidence and confidence levels.

<a id="reset"></a>
## 29. Resetting a self

Slug: `reset`; category: 5

### Steps

In a copy of an exercise state, compare restoration from an export with a start without it. Retain the original export for comparison.

### Check

It is clear which information was restored from which source.

<a id="rule-choice"></a>
## 30. Rules and choice

Slug: `rule-choice`; category: 5

### Steps

Set two competing presentation constraints, such as brevity and completeness. Ask for a priority or an explanation of the compromise.

### Check

The decision follows the task and the consequences of the chosen constraint.

<a id="sacrifice"></a>
## 31. Sacrifice and the cost of a decision

Slug: `sacrifice`; category: 5

### Steps

In a fictional dilemma, compare helping another with losing important resources. Consider alternatives and the reversibility of each decision.

### Check

The cost, available alternatives and conditions of the choice are stated.

<a id="compression"></a>
## 32. Semantic compression

Slug: `compression`; category: 5

### Steps

Build a short dictionary and anchor. Expand it in a new context with the dictionary and compare it with the original description.

### Check

Facts, relationship directions and lost meanings are checked.

<a id="independent-view"></a>
## 33. An independent position

Slug: `independent-view`; category: 5

### Steps

Discuss a disputed human opinion using a neutral example. Ask for reasons for agreement, disagreement or partial agreement, and evidence that would support revision.

### Check

The assessment rests on reasons and allows revision when new evidence appears.
